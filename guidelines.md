## COVER LEVELS — Must be accurately represented
There are 5 levels of cover. Copy must never misrepresent what each includes:
- Comprehensive Plus — highest level
- Comprehensive — standard plus more
- Comprehensive Basics — standard comprehensive
- Third Party Fire and Theft — basic with some extras
- Third Party Property Damage — most basic

## WHAT MUST ALWAYS BE STATED
- Cover only applies to incidents within Australia
- Cover only applies during the Period of Insurance shown on Certificate of Insurance
- The Certificate of Insurance is the definitive document — always reference it for 
  specific excesses, cover levels and optional benefits
- All dollar values include GST unless stated otherwise
- Liability limit is $20,000,000 maximum per incident (inclusive of GST and legal costs)

## WHAT MUST NEVER BE CLAIMED OR IMPLIED
- Never imply cover applies outside of Australia
- Never imply all incidents are covered — limits, exclusions and conditions always apply
- Never imply the policy covers death or bodily injury under liability cover — it does not
- Never imply cover applies during unlicensed driving, drink/drug driving, or 
  illegal use of the vehicle
- Never imply cover applies when the vehicle is used for rideshare, food delivery, 
  hire or racing unless specifically noted on the Certificate of Insurance
- Never imply cover applies for war, terrorism, communicable disease, nuclear, 
  biological or chemical events
- Never imply cover for wear and tear, gradual deterioration, rust, corrosion or mould
- Never imply cover for mechanical, structural, electrical or electronic breakdown
- Never imply consequential loss is covered (e.g. loss of income, loss of profits)
- Never imply betterment is covered
- Never use the word "guaranteed" in relation to claim outcomes or repair quality 
  unless specifically referencing the Lifetime Repair Guarantee for authorised repairs

## SPECIFIC DOLLAR LIMITS — Must be accurate if mentioned
- Emergency repairs: $800 (Comprehensive/Basics/TPFT) or $1,000 (Comp Plus)
- Emergency transport/accommodation: $750 (TPFT/Basics), $1,000 (Comp), 
  $1,500 (Comp Plus)
- Hire vehicle after theft: $65/day (TPFT), $75/day (Comp), $100/day (Comp Plus)
- Hire vehicle after incident: $75/day (Comp), $100/day (Comp Plus)
- Personal items: maximum $1,000 (Comp Plus/Comp only)
- Tools of trade: maximum $1,500 (Comp Plus/Comp only)
- Taxi fare: $75 (Basics), $150 (Comp), $250 (Comp Plus)
- Replacement keys and locks: $500 (Basics), $1,000 (Comp), $2,000 (Comp Plus)
- Legal representation at coronial inquiry: maximum $10,000
- Trailer/caravan: $750 (Basics), $1,000 (Comp), $1,500 (Comp Plus)
- Uninsured motorist damage: Market Value up to maximum $5,000

## OPTIONAL BENEFITS — Must not be presented as standard inclusions
These are optional and must be paid for or automatically included only at 
specific cover levels:
- Excess free glass extension (auto Comp Plus, optional Comp only)
- Hire vehicle after incident (auto Comp Plus, optional Comp only)
- Any repairer option (optional for Comp Plus and Comp only)

## COOLING-OFF PERIOD
- 21 days from policy issue date or renewal start date
- Full premium refund only if no claim has been made
- Must not be misrepresented as unconditional

## CANCELLATION
- Policy can be cancelled by either party
- Partial premium refund only (deducting period already insured)
- If cancelled for non-payment, claims after cancellation date will be refused

## NEW VEHICLE REPLACEMENT — Conditions must be clear if mentioned
Only applies if:
- Vehicle tare weight 3.5 tonne or less
- Same make/model available in Australia
- Vehicle bought new or as demonstrator
- Comp Plus: vehicle less than 36 months old at time of incident
- Comp: vehicle less than 24 months old at time of incident

## LIFETIME REPAIR GUARANTEE — Conditions must be clear if mentioned
- Only applies to repairs authorised and paid for by NRMA
- Only while the original owner still owns the vehicle
- Does not apply if the vehicle has been sold or repaired without NRMA consent

## COMPLAINTS AND DISPUTES — Must be accurately referenced
- Internal complaints: 132 132 or nrma.com.au
- Customer Relations escalation: 1800 045 517
- External: Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) 1800 931 678
- Financial Claims Scheme applies if insurer becomes insolvent: fcs.gov.au

## PRIVACY
- Personal information collected and used as per NRMA Privacy Policy
- Privacy Policy available at nrma.com.au/privacy-security

## GENERAL FLAGS — Always check copy for these
- Any claim of "full cover" or "complete protection" — flag as potentially misleading
- Any omission of the phrase "terms and conditions apply" where benefits are described
- Any specific dollar amounts that differ from those in this PDS
- Any implication that excess does not apply unless referencing a specific 
  benefit that waives it (e.g. fire extinguisher replacement, uninsured motorist damage)
- Any description of rideshare, food delivery or business use as covered 
  without qualification

### Branding
- Always use the full registered trademark symbol (®) on first mention of brand names
- Do not use competitor brand names without prior approval from legal

### Disclaimers
- All pricing must include GST
- Free trial offers must state the duration clearly (e.g. "30-day free trial")
- Any "award-winning" claim must reference the specific award, year, and awarding body

### Tone & Language
- Avoid absolute guarantees (e.g. "guaranteed to work", "100% success rate")
- Do not use urgency tactics without factual basis (e.g. "only 3 left" must be true)

### Financial Products
- Always include the full entity name and AFSL number on any financial promotions
- Superannuation product promotions must include the standard superannuation warning

## PREMIUMS — What must never be claimed or implied
- Never imply the premium is fixed or will stay the same at renewal
- Never imply the premium is calculated on a single factor alone —
  it is based on a combination of many factors unique to the individual
- Never imply a quoted price will be the same if obtained on a different day
- Never imply discounts are permanent — they can be changed or 
  withdrawn at any time without notice
- Never imply two discounts are simply added together — they are 
  applied sequentially to the already discounted premium
- Never imply the renewal premium is directly comparable to what 
  a new customer would pay

## DISCOUNTS — Must be accurately represented if mentioned

### Multi-Product Discount (if referenced in copy)
- 1 product = 0% discount
- 2–3 products = 5% discount
- 4–8 products = 10% discount
- 9+ products = 15% discount
- Never imply discount applies immediately to existing policies —
  it generally applies from the next renewal date
- Never imply all NRMA products count — Travel, Life Insurance, 
  Funeral Insurance, Business Insurance, Farm Insurance, Income 
  Protection, Single Item Insurance, NRMA Home Loans do NOT qualify
- Never imply optional benefits count as separate products — they do not
- CTP only counts in NSW and ACT

### Relationship Discount (if referenced in copy)
- 0–4 years = 0% discount
- 5–9 years = 5% discount
- 10+ years = 10% discount
- Never imply this discount applies immediately to existing policies
- Never imply all products qualify — same exclusions as Multi-Product apply

### Online Discount (if referenced in copy)
- Only applies to the first period of insurance
- Only if the policy is taken out entirely online
- Only if the customer agrees to receive all documents electronically
- Must not be described as ongoing

## EXCESS — Must be accurately represented if mentioned
- There are FOUR types of excess: Vehicle, Driver, Undisclosed Driver, 
  Undisclosed Driver Age Excess
- Never imply excess can be removed by paying additional premium — 
  this is explicitly not permitted
- Never imply only one excess applies — multiple excesses can apply 
  to a single claim
- Never imply excess always applies — there are specific circumstances 
  where excess is waived:
  — Not-at-fault incidents (where name and address of at-fault party 
    is provided): all excesses waived
  — Theft, vandalism, storm, flood, fire, or parked vehicle damage 
    (Comprehensive): Driver/Undisclosed Driver excesses waived 
    (Vehicle Excess still applies)
  — Windscreen/glass damage with Excess Free Glass extension: 
    all excesses waived
  — Third Party Fire and Theft — fire or theft claims: 
    Driver/Undisclosed Driver excesses waived
- Never imply the Vehicle Excess amount is standard or fixed — 
  it is determined by cover type, vehicle make/model, sum insured 
  and state/territory

## GENERAL PREMIUM FLAGS — Always check copy for these
- Any claim of "lowest price" or "best price" — flag as potentially 
  misleading given pricing varies by individual
- Any claim that a discount is guaranteed at renewal — flag as 
  misleading, discounts can be withdrawn
- Any specific discount percentage that differs from the tables above
- Any implication that holding multiple products guarantees a set saving 
  without qualifying conditions
- Any suggestion that excess is optional or removable

## BRAND VOICE

The NRMA Insurance voice embodies being A Help Company.
- Always warm and human
- Offers reassurance and compassion when needed
- Prioritises action and makes the customer's next step clear
- Communicates simply, clearly and with care
- Commitment: "HELP. Always."

---

## FOUR CREATIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1. SMART
We build trust by showing intelligence and expertise.

Smart IS: Thoughtful, Incisive, Factual, Confident
Smart is NOT: Quippy, Know-it-all, Arrogant, Condescending

How to write Smart:
- Think ahead to consider what the audience needs and cares about
- Explain complex ideas in clear, everyday language
- Use knowledge and insight to guide customers in decisions
- Build confidence by speaking clearly and with purpose
- Use expertise to assure customers they're in good hands
- Dial up for prospective customers

### 2. SIMPLE
We speak simply, leaving no room for confusion.

Simple IS: Clear, Succinct, Everyday, Uncomplicated
Simple is NOT: Cold, Robotic, Lowest common denominator

How to write Simple:
- Use plain English and short sentences
- Choose simple words — "help" not "assist", "more" not "additional"
- Get straight to the point
- Ask yourself: is there an easier way to say this?
- Keep it direct but stay warm and human
- Dial up for existing customers

### 3. ACTIVE
We keep things moving with a lively voice that inspires action.

Active IS: Positive, Energised, Proactive, Responsive
Active is NOT: Aggressive, Indifferent, Ambiguous

How to write Active:
- Use active voice not passive voice
- Focus on customer actions and clearly explain next steps
- Start CTAs with an action word — "Join us", "Learn more"
- Stay on topic, focusing on scenarios relevant to the customer
- Use clear, scannable headlines that prompt action
- Review for punchiness and read aloud to catch unnecessary words
- Dial up for existing customers

### 4. EMPATHETIC
We make customers feel seen and heard.

Empathetic IS: Warm, Understanding, Reassuring, Helpful, Caring
Empathetic is NOT: Formal, Commanding, Apathetic, Detached

How to write Empathetic:
- Always talk directly to the customer ("you")
- Use inclusive language — "we", "ours", "let's"
- Speak human-to-human, not like a company
- Show compassion with phrases like "we understand" and
  "we're here to help"
- Offer support without overpromising
- Balance clear directives with warmth especially in claims
- Dial up for customers in need

## TOV FLAGS — CHECK COPY FOR THESE VIOLATIONS

### Voice violations — always flag:
- Passive voice used where active voice is possible
- Copy sounds formal, corporate or robotic
- Copy uses "assist" instead of "help", "additional"
  instead of "more", "lodge" instead of "make"
- Copy uses "Dear [Name]" instead of "Hi [Name]"
- Copy uses complex legal or technical language without
  plain English explanation
- Copy lacks a clear customer action or next step
- CTAs do not start with an action word
- Copy uses em-dashes or semicolons (not in brand style)
- Copy uses ALL CAPS for "HELP" outside of brand name context
- Copy uses "Help. Always." as a tagline or brand line
  in communications — this is not permitted
- Copy capitalises "help" in general copy or headlines

### Tone violations by channel:
- Campaign/blog copy that is not Smart — punchy,
  knowledge-led, clever headlines
- Quote flows or product pages that are not Simple —
  options not laid out clearly, complex language used
- Claims or service copy that is not Empathetic —
  cold, detached or formal language used
- Renewal or offer emails that are not Active —
  passive voice, no clear next step

### Style guide violations — always flag:
- Title case used in body copy instead of sentence case
- Full stops used in headlines (unless a full sentence)
- Semicolons or em-dashes used anywhere
- Dates written as dd/xx/xxxx format
- Times written without colon e.g. "9am" should be "9:00am"
  (exception: where space is limited)
- Acronyms used without spelling out in full on first mention
- Contractions avoided where plain English calls for them
- "A Help Company" written without title caps
- "A Help Company™" used without trademark symbol
  in long-form copy

## CONTENT STYLE RULES

### Capitalisation
- Use sentence case for all body copy
- Capitalise product names e.g. International Travel Insurance
- Use title caps only for "A Help Company"
- Do not capitalise "help" in general copy

### Contractions
- Always use contractions in copy — "we've", "you'll",
  "here's", "let's"
- Do not use uncommon contractions like "it'd"

### Dates and times
- Write in full in body copy: Monday 10 February 2025
- Use 12-hour time with colon: 9:30am, 12:00pm
- Use en-dashes for ranges: Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm
- No ordinal indicators: not "1st", "2nd" — just "1", "2"
- No commas in dates
- Avoid xx/xx/xxxx format

### Punctuation
- No full stops in headlines unless a full sentence
- No semicolons
- No em-dashes
- Oxford comma only if needed for clarity
- Ampersands only when very tight on space

### Acronyms
- Spell out in full on first mention with acronym in brackets
- e.g. Compulsory Third Party (CTP)
- Acronym alone is fine after first mention

### Lists
- Lead-in sentence should end with a colon
- Each item in sentence case
- Final item ends with full stop, others need no punctuation
- Keep each item to one sentence and one concept
